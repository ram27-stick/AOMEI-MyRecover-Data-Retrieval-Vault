**Because deleted does not mean gone.** Every storage medium is a palimpsest — a surface written over, time and again, each layer whispering the faint echo of what once lived there. ## 🏺 The Philosophy of the Second Look

Most recovery tools market themselves on speed. Revenant Vault markets itself on **attention**. We believe the second look — the deliberate, unhurried re-examination of a drive — is where real restoration happens.

Think of it like forensic paleontology. You don't bulldoze a dig site hoping fossils pop out. You map the grid, you label the quadrant, you brush away layers. Our engine mirrors that discipline:

- **Non-destructive scanning** — the drive is observed, not rewritten.
- **Read-first architecture** — every action favors interpretation over intervention.
- **Transparent logs** — you see exactly what was found and why.
- **Human-in-the-loop confirmation** — nothing is restored without your explicit blessing.

This is not a race. It is archaeology.

### 🧩 Deep-Scan Reconstruction
The engine doesn't just list lost files — it rebuilds them from fragments. Partial JPEGs are stitched, truncated documents are reflowed, and video containers are reassembled from their scattered atoms. If a file survived as a shadow, we try to give it a body.

### 🗂️ Instant Visual Preview
Before you commit to anything, you see it. Thumbnails populate in real time as the scan progresses, letting you sort the wheat from the chaff by eye rather than by filename alone.

### 🧠 Heuristic File Naming
Lost names are a special kind of heartbreak. Our naming engine cross-references metadata signatures, timestamps, and known application patterns to suggest plausible original names — not a guess, but an educated whisper.

### 🗺️ Drive Territory Mapping
Every scan produces a visual heatmap of the drive's readable regions, showing where the density of recoverable material is highest. You learn your own hardware as you go.

### ⏳ Timeline Reconstruction
Files are grouped by likely creation and modification windows, letting you reconstruct the *story* of a folder rather than just its inventory.

### 🔐 Integrity Verification
Before any file is considered recovered, a checksum comparison confirms that the reconstructed data matches the original signature where available. No phantom recoveries.

### 🛡️ Read-Only Guarantee
The engine never writes to the source drive during scanning. Your evidence remains untouched until you choose a destination.

### 📤 Export-Ready Bundles
Recovered items can be exported as organized bundles — by type, by date, or by confidence score — directly into a location of your choosing.

## 📖 The Recovery Workflow, Explained Like a Story

Picture a small library. A shelf collapses. The librarian does not sweep the floor and throw everything away — she first photographs the mess, then catalogs what she can identify, then carefully re-shelves.

Revenant Vault follows three acts:

**Act I — The Survey.** You point the tool at a drive. It performs a census: partition tables, file system markers, cluster boundaries. No changes are made.

**Act II — The Excavation.** The deep-scan engine walks through the readable territory, cataloging every candidate signature it recognizes: the opening bytes of a photo, the header of a document, the container tags of a video. Each candidate is logged with a confidence score.

**Act III — The Restoration.** You review the catalog, mark the keepers, and export them to a safe location. Every recovered item carries a verification stamp.

That's it. No ceremony, no jargon, no leap of faith.

## ⚙️ Under the Hood: How the Engine Thinks

Revenant Vault is not a single program. It's a small parliament of cooperating modules:

- **Sigil Parser** — recognizes file headers and trailers across hundreds of formats.
- **Cluster Weaver** — reconstructs fragmented chains by analyzing spatial proximity and timing metadata.
- **Confidence Oracle** — assigns each candidate a believability score based on signature integrity and reconstructability.
- **Naming Seer** — suggests plausible filenames using pattern recognition and application metadata.
- **Vault Renderer** — turns all of this into a legible, searchable interface.

Each module is open, each is documented, and each can be improved by the community. We consider this a public library of recovery knowledge, not a black box.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Revenant Vault a replacement for my backup strategy?**
Absolutely not. It is a second chance, not a first defense. Keep backups. Love backups. Backups are the only true immortality for data.

**Will it work on a drive that's been formatted?**
Often, yes — formatting frequently rewrites only the directory structure, leaving file contents intact. Results vary depending on how much new data has been written since.

**Do I need to be a technician?**
No. The interface is built for ordinary humans who happen to be having a very bad day.

**Does it modify my drive?**
The scanning engine is strictly read-only. Writes only occur when you explicitly export recovered files to a destination you choose.

**What about solid-state drives with TRIM enabled?**
SSDs present unique challenges because of wear-leveling and TRIM. Revenant Vault will still attempt recovery, but the window of opportunity is often narrower. Success depends heavily on how quickly you act.
